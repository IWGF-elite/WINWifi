# WinWiFi Security Auditor — animated product walkthrough

## Creative direction
A confident, international product film built around a real browser session of the WinWiFi application. The product screen remains live throughout: real navigation, searches, selections, panels, and report states, driven by a visible pointer. Camera moves punch in to the control being used, then pull back on navigation. Use the application's navy, blue, and teal palette with a restrained cinematic grade, clean match cuts, and precise click sound design. No offline-demo labels, development-status language, or static screenshot montage.

## Audience and purpose
Security teams and technical stakeholders seeing WinWiFi Security Auditor for the first time. Show the workflow and the information the application presents, with enough detail to feel like a real product walkthrough.

## Story
Hook: a cursor opens the Command Center, revealing the assessment workspace.
Proof: navigate through network inventory, access-point details, RF spectrum, threat triage, investigations, packet forensics, handshakes, and reporting.
Payoff: return to the overview and land on the product name with a concise closing promise.

## On-screen walkthrough, approximately 68 seconds
1. Command Center, 0–7s: pointer enters and selects the network posture summary; controlled camera push to priority cards.
2. Network inventory, 7–18s: click Networks; pointer moves through search and filter controls; focus on a selected access point and its technical detail panel.
3. RF Spectrum, 18–25s: click RF Spectrum; compare the band controls and channel footprints; click a plotted network to show selection response.
4. Threats and Investigations, 25–36s: open Threats, focus the rogue indicator, then open the associated investigation and its status/evidence area.
5. Packet forensics, 36–46s: open Forensics & PCAP; change the frame filter, select a packet, and punch in on the decoded layer and raw-byte pane.
6. Handshake review, 46–54s: open Handshakes and focus the EAPOL record and export action.
7. Reporting, 54–63s: open Reports; switch executive/technical views or focus the report export controls and remediation summary.
8. Close, 63–68s: pull out to the application shell, settle on WinWiFi Security Auditor and the closing line.

## Motion rules
- Record at 1920×1080, 30 fps, landscape.
- Use five clear interaction types: pointer travel, click response, focus zoom, real content change, and fast match-cut/pullback between application areas.
- Camera travels take 0.35–0.65s with an ease-out settle. Hold the actual interface while a control responds.
- Pointer is 48–64px at 1080p, with a small cyan focus halo and a brief click ripple; no decorative looping or generic floating panels.
- Add short restrained UI clicks and soft transition accents, mixed below narration.
- Keep callout text sparse and tied to the active screen. Use the application itself as the visual source.

## Narration
Use the accompanying narration-script.txt. Natural neutral international English, measured and authoritative, around 145–155 words per minute. No claims that the software is under development, no offline-demo wording, and no unsupported claim of password cracking or active attacks.

## Audio
Use the user's selected HeyGen voice route after sign-in completes. Add a restrained cinematic-electronic bed with a subtle pulse and clear headroom for voice. Keep the narration forward; the music and UI sounds should support, not compete.
